Emergency flash flood relief

2026
ASHIKA Development Associates, funded by WFP · Rangamati
Problem
Flooding hit five unions in Rangamati hard. Households needed food and energy supplies quickly, and there was no record yet of who was affected or what each family still lacked.
What I did
Helped distribute rice, oil, dry food, and energy biscuits under the WFP-funded relief programme. I recorded each household's situation on paper forms and entered the data afterwards, so that relief could be directed by need.
Role
Volunteer and coordinator among the affected households, as part of a team led by Ratna Jyoti Chakma.
Result
The distribution reached about 400 households across the five affected unions.

Food habits & climate change workshop

2023, 2024
RMSTU Indigenous Students' Union · Chittagong Hill Tracts
Problem
Mothers and adolescent girls (kishories) in the Chittagong Hill Tracts had few structured sessions on how local food habits relate to nutrition and to climate change.
What I did
Facilitated a three-day workshop on the food habits of the Chittagong Hill Tracts and the impact of climate change, organized by the RMSTU Indigenous Students' Union. The sessions covered what the region's indigenous food habits offer for nutrition, and how climate change affects food and jhum cultivation. Printed material was produced from the sessions.
Leadership
Facilitated the sessions directly rather than assisting someone else who led them.
Result
The workshop ran across 2023 and 2024, with 20 to 50 mothers and kishories attending each time.

The Chittagong Hill Tracts differ from the plains districts of Bangladesh in what people eat and how they farm. A published review of traditional food plants in the region describes indigenous livelihoods as still dominated by subsistence agriculture that combines jhum (shifting cultivation), plough cultivation, and mixed fruit gardens. The workshop asked what this local food base offers for the nutrition of mothers and adolescent girls.

Climate change bears on the same food base. An ethnographic study in Kutukchhari and Sapchhari unions of Rangamati Sadar found that climate change and human causes together threaten food security and disrupt farming, and that people are being pushed to change their traditional jhum cultivation. In work reported by UNESCO, a youth-led study of 72 Tripura women in Simana Para, Khagrachari found that erratic weather disrupts jhum cultivation, water access, health, and food security.

Participants discussed how food and climate change are linked, and how changes in jhum affect what is grown and eaten.

Sources: Garai (2025), Wild 2(2):13; UNESCO, youth-led climate research in the Chittagong Hill Tracts; Traditional food plants and agro-biodiversity in the Chittagong Hill Tracts.
